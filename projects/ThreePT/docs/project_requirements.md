# Course project requirements (our summary)

Summary of what the professor asked for in the Week 12 guideline slides, the lecture recording,
and the GitHub submission email (2026-11-21). Written in our own words.

## What the project is

- A **four-week** team project about a concrete **Business Intelligence decision** that uses **social computing** data
  (people's digital interactions: reports, reviews, comments, relationships).
- The team must collect data, build a result, and **demonstrate** it.
- The professor stressed: four weeks is short, so the team must set a realistic scope.
  "We failed because we had only four weeks" is not accepted.

## Milestones

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Week 12 (Nov 17) | Proposal presentation and feedback (done) |
| Weeks 12–15 | Collect data, implement, analyze, evaluate |
| Week 15 (Dec 8) | Final presentation of results, **including a live demo** |
| Week 16 | Submit source code, demo video, and final report |

## Team rules

- Up to five students. One team leader coordinates milestones, meetings, and progress tracking.
- Every member must make a substantive contribution (no free riding).
- For each member: name, specific role, expected output.
- Explain how teammates review and integrate each other's work.

## What the proposal / final work must cover

1. **Background and motivation**: the problem, who has it, why it matters, evidence it is worth studying.
2. **BI relevance**: the stakeholder who uses the result, the decision they face, what data changes that decision,
   and the expected action after the analysis (as a scenario). The professor suggested thinking with a Lean Canvas.
3. **Originality and technical challenge**: what is distinctive (question, data, method, or application),
   the technical challenge, and a comparison with a simple baseline or existing approach.
   Common topics (e.g. "Netflix-style movie recommendation") were given as an example of low originality.
4. **Four-week feasibility**: a minimum result that can be built and demonstrated, essential work vs. optional
   extensions, the largest implementation dependency, and a smaller fallback if it fails (risk management).
5. **Data sources and collection**: each source and its key fields, whether access is available, how data is
   collected or generated, permissions, privacy constraints, missing fields, and the expected data scale.
6. **Evaluation plan (marked as especially important)**: define the primary outcome before building,
   choose a baseline, use quality measures that fit the task, explain how the result helps the stakeholder.
7. **Expected results and limitations (stressed)**: the prototype or analysis to deliver, findings stated as
   hypotheses, what the data cannot establish, and a practical risk that could change the plan.
8. **Collaboration workspace**: a shared tool such as Notion for tasks, meeting notes, decisions, and files,
   with the professor added as an observer (done).
9. **Schedule and accountability**: a schedule covering data, implementation, evaluation, and final presentation;
   an owner and target date for each major task; record progress and blockers; update the schedule when plans change.
